Selected work

Approvals framework, KAYAK for Business

2024 to present · architect and sole backend engineer

A corporate travel platform is only usable by a large employer if it can enforce the employer's travel policy. The approval system I replaced blocked travellers before they could book, and when I analysed its production data it turned out to be failing as a control: very few requests were ever declined, and many were never acted on at all.

I designed the replacement. Travellers book normally, the approval request is created and routed at checkout, and a declined booking is cancelled automatically inside the airline's free cancellation window. Administrators choose what happens when an approver does not respond, so no booking is left pending indefinitely.

  • Launched in February 2025 on a programme of about ten people. Both earlier approval mechanisms were retired, and this is now the only approval method in the product.
  • Later projects by other engineers and teams, including multi-level approvals and backup approvers, extend the design rather than replace it.
  • In 2026 I added an engine that checks bookings made by human travel agents against the employer's policy. Those bookings had bypassed policy entirely, a gap that had been open for three years.

Public documentation: KAYAK for Business Help Center, Approvals.

Trip reporting fields, KAYAK for Business

2024 to present · designer and owner

Companies need every booking tagged with their own accounting and compliance data: a cost centre, a project code, a reason for travel, a reason for booking outside policy. KAYAK used to meet each of these requests with custom code for one customer at a time, and a single field could take months.

I designed one general framework instead. A company's administrators define the fields they need, travellers answer them at checkout, and the answers travel with the booking into the company's own reporting and finance systems.

  • New reporting requirements are now configuration, set up by the customer's administrators without engineering work.
  • The framework has grown from six standard field types to twelve, and other teams have built post-booking editing and event reporting on the same data model.
